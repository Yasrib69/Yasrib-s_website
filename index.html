About me

A foundation in research, engineering, and education.

I’m building toward a research career that combines careful evaluation, practical software development, and an understanding of the people who use technology.

Who I am

I’m Yasrib Yasir Farook, a Computer Science – Applied undergraduate at Eastern Michigan University. My experience spans undergraduate research, software development, university IT support, programming instruction, and student leadership. These roles have shaped how I approach computing: investigate the question, build a workable solution, evaluate it carefully, and explain it clearly.

My research experience

With Dr. Siyuan Jiang, I work on generative AI in software requirements and computing education. I contributed as second author to an ACM SIGCSE Virtual 2026 publication analyzing 406 user stories from 48 students at two universities. I am also designing a structured GenAI training intervention and contributing to a systematic review of disability and accessibility in computer science education.

With Dr. Yunting Yin, I work on cardiac ultrasound video analysis using EchoNet-Dynamic. My contributions include linking video and clinical data, validating ventricular tracing records, and configuring PyTorch segmentation and video-model workflows. This work has strengthened my experience with research data, computer vision, and GPU-based experimentation.

What I bring to a team

I combine research methods with hands-on engineering: Python and PyTorch workflows, full-stack prototypes, API integration, cloud deployment, and troubleshooting university systems. Teaching more than 100 students and mentoring neurodivergent learners have helped me communicate technical ideas and adapt instruction to different needs.

My next step

I’m seeking PhD opportunities for Fall 2027 in human–AI collaboration, AI for software engineering, medical AI, accessibility, and computing education. I want to deepen my technical and methodological skills while contributing to research that is useful to people. I also welcome engineering roles and collaborations where research, implementation, and thoughtful evaluation meet.

Structured GenAI training for better software requirements

Does teaching deliberate LLM interaction improve the requirements people write?

Ongoing study · Training and study design
Read research details +

Research context

A controlled empirical study asking whether structured GenAI training improves how participants communicate with LLMs and the quality of the software requirements they produce.

Study design & my contributions

  • Designed a training intervention around context provision, prompt specificity, AI-output evaluation, and iterative refinement.
  • Created control and intervention conditions, scenarios, pre/post assessments, evaluation methodology, and IRB materials.
  • Studies whether users can recognize plausible but unsupported AI-generated assumptions, requirements, and scope.
  • Uses a Generate → Question → Evaluate → Improve workflow to teach more deliberate interaction with AI.
Advisor: Dr. Siyuan Jiang · Eastern Michigan University
Research status

This study examines a training intervention. Findings from the intervention are not presented here.

Jul. 2026 — PresentMedical AI

Deep learning for cardiac ultrasound video

Building the data and model workflows needed to study heart function from video.

10,030 videos · 425,010 tracing records
Read research details +

Research context

Deep learning research using cardiac ultrasound videos to study left-ventricular segmentation, cardiac motion, and ejection-fraction prediction.

Pipeline, models & my contributions

  • Built a Python pipeline linking 10,030 AVI videos with clinical metadata and 425,010 volume-tracing records.
  • Validated matching tracing data for 10,024 videos and identified unmatched cases for review.
  • Configured and tested PyTorch workflows for DeepLabV3 segmentation and R(2+1)D video-based EF prediction.
  • Worked across local development, Colab, and NVIDIA A10 GPU infrastructure using CUDA-enabled PyTorch.
Advisor: Dr. Yunting Yin · Eastern Michigan University
Research status

Current contributions concern data validation and model workflows. Clinical performance results are not presented here.

How GenAI changes student-written user stories

An empirical analysis of requirements written with and without AI assistance.

406 user stories · 48 students · 2 universities
Read research details +

Research context

Empirical research on how GenAI affects software-requirement quality in undergraduate software engineering education.

Methods, findings & my contributions

  • Analyzed 406 user stories from 48 students at two U.S. public universities.
  • Applied INVEST-based measures to compare requirements produced with and without GenAI assistance.
  • Observed improvements in testability and structure while identifying limitations in decomposition and scope judgment.
  • Contributed as second author to a peer-reviewed ACM SIGCSE Virtual 2026 publication.
Advisor: Dr. Siyuan Jiang · Eastern Michigan University
Research status

The analysis observed improvements in structure and testability, alongside limitations in decomposition and scope judgment.

Accessibility · Senior Research Project

Speaker-Anchored Live Captioning for DHH Students

Live captions anchored to a speaker’s face for Deaf and Hard of Hearing students.

Explore project +

What I built & why

An in-progress applied research project exploring AR as assistive technology for Deaf and Hard of Hearing students. The prototype captures live audio, transcribes speech using a streaming STT service, detects a single speaker, and anchors captions below the speaker’s face so the user does not need to constantly shift attention to a fixed caption box.

Implementation & scope

The current scope intentionally focuses on one speaker. Multi-speaker detection, speaker switching, and more advanced AR behavior are planned as future work.
